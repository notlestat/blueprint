Which look should Blueprint have?

Three directions for the whiteboard. Each one shows the same board: three artboards from a made-up booking site, with two open notes and one resolved. They differ in where the tool's controls live and how a note connects to the thing it is about.

These are static mockups with a little real behaviour. Hover or tab onto a note or a pin in A and C. Click a frame or use the arrow keys in B. Pan and zoom are not built yet.

A · Drawing sheet My pick

The board is a technical drawing. Every control and the list of notes sit in one title block in the corner, the way an engineering drawing carries its details. Artboards have dimension lines and crop marks. A note is a red triangle with a line that runs out to its text in the margin.

What you getAll notes are readable at once without opening anything. The title block doubles as a status panel: sheets, open notes, last change.
What it costsSmall capital letters in a monospace font are slower to read. Leader lines get tangled once an artboard has more than about five notes.
Depth and motionSheets lift off the grid with a soft shadow. Leader lines draw themselves in. The scale bar and dimensions update as you zoom.

B · Light table Dark, one frame in focus

The board is a photographer's light table. The frame you are working on is lit and the others fall back into the dark. Notes are yellow wax-pencil rings drawn round the thing you mean. A filmstrip along the bottom replaces the sidebar.

What you getThe strongest focus on one design at a time. A ring shows the exact area you mean, which a dot cannot. The filmstrip is a fast way to jump between frames.
What it costsDimmed frames are hard to compare side by side. You only see the notes for the lit frame. A dark surround changes how light designs look.
Depth and motionThe light moves to the frame you pick and the frame lifts slightly. The wax ring draws on. Other frames dim.

C · Proof Notes in the margin

The board is a set of printer's proofs. Each artboard has a large number and its own margin, and notes sit in that margin level with their pin, like marks on a page proof. Your words are black and the agent's replies are blue. The controls are one line of type across the top.

What you getThe easiest notes to read and reply to. The black and blue split shows who said what at a glance. Very little interface on screen.
What it costsEvery artboard needs a margin, so fewer fit on screen. The big numerals compete with the designs. Two notes at the same height have to be pushed apart.
Depth and motionMostly flat by intent. A hairline runs from a note to its pin when you point at either one. The numeral tucks behind the sheet.
